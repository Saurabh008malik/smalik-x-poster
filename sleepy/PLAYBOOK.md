# The Sleepy Biographer: production playbook (how video 1, Benjamin Franklin, was made)

Repeat these steps for every video. Update this file when something improves. Owner: Saurabh. Channel: The Sleepy Biographer (@TheSleepyBiographerHQ, ID UClAFUvhMXPSbgGRavFb1T9w, account saurabh.malik008@gmail.com).

## Rules that never change

5. Repeat what works: after each video, read the numbers and copy the winning pattern (see step 12).

## Pipeline (about 4 to 5 hours of compute per video)
1. **Pick the topic with data.** vidIQ keyword research and outliers (credits: vidiq_keyword_research, vidiq_outliers, vidiq_youtube_search). Prefer a famous person, well documented, with proven sleep-biography videos of 1.5 to 3 hours that got 100K+ views. Score the title with vidiq_score_title. Franklin title: "Benjamin Franklin: The Printer Who Lit Up the World | Biography for Sleep".
2. **Study the proven videos** (vidiq_video_watch is 25 credits, async; poll with vidiq_job_poll; or transcripts). What worked: deep British male RP voice at 90 to 115 words per minute, quiet fireplace bed, warm painterly images with slow zoom, chapters.
3. **Research brief** (brief.md): 12 chapters, timeline, primary and reliable sources (autobiography on Gutenberg, Founders Online, national archives or museums, Britannica), plus a "facts to treat with care" list. Claude's shell cannot reach most sites, so use WebSearch and WebFetch; many sites (youtube.com, wikipedia.org) are blocked in WebFetch, so search for other sources.
4. **Write the script in 12 chapters** (script/ch01.txt to ch12.txt): each about 800 to 1,250 words, "## Chapter N: Title", plain paragraphs, no markdown, no lists. Calm, simple sentences, soft hand-off at the end of each chapter, chapter 12 ends with a gentle goodnight. Numbers and dates spelled out in words ("seventeen hundred and fifty-two"). Frame imagined scenes as imagined. Include honest shadows (for Franklin: slavery, William, the Cockpit). Total about 12,500 words gives about 1 h 55 min.
5. **Fact-check every chapter** against sources and fix before narration. Typical catches: wrong month or year, anecdotes whose source is a later account ("according to the best-known account"), claims about people nobody documented, invented details (a cat by the fire). No direct quotes except a few short lines from the subject's own public-domain writing, and never fabricated quotes.
6. **Narration** (free, open source): Kokoro TTS via `kokoro_onnx` (files in /home/claude/tts: kokoro-v1.0.onnx, voices-v1.0.bin). Voice `bm_george`, speed 0.74, `lang="en-gb"`. Script: /home/claude/franklin/narrate.py renders sentence by sentence, 1.0 s pause after sentences, 1.6 s after paragraphs, 2.5 s before chapter headings; lines starting `[NARRATOR` are skipped. Render with render_all.py in two parallel processes (odd and even chapters): about 10 to 12 minutes per chapter, about 65 minutes total on 2 cores. Result about 110 wpm.
7. **Images.** The sandbox cannot reach Wikimedia, LoC or the Met. Use Saurabh's Chrome (claude-in-chrome tools): open commons.wikimedia.org, then run JS that searches the Commons API (origin=*), keeps only Public domain, CC0 and "No restrictions" files at least 1000 px wide, fetches 1280 px thumbnails, builds ONE zip in the page (store-only zip writer) and downloads it once to Downloads (Chrome blocks multiple automatic downloads, so never download 90 files one by one). Stage the zip into the sandbox with device_stage_files and unzip into /home/claude/franklin/img. The zip contains sources.json (title, page URL, licence, artist) which feeds the description credits. Make contact sheets (Pillow) to choose and map about 6 to 16 images per chapter (CH_IMGS in assemble.py).
8. **Audio mix** (assemble.py audio): 6 s lead, all chapters, 10 s tail; synthesized fire bed (fire.py, our own, copyright free) at 0.05 under the voice at 0.9, loudnorm I=-20 LUFS, AAC 192k. Saves timeline.json with chapter durations (used for the description timestamps).
9. **Video** (assemble.py video): 1920x1080, 24 fps. Each image is composed once at 2400x1350 (blurred dark background, warm sepia grade, soft shadow, vignette), then a slow zoompan (12 fps then 24 fps, about 52 seconds per image, alternating zoom in, pan, zoom out, closer detail for repeats), fade through black 1.5 s per clip. Title card for the first 6 s. Rendered in 2 parallel ffmpeg workers (preset veryfast, crf 24); concat and mux with the audio, faststart. About 40 to 60 minutes. Check the output with ffprobe (duration equals audio) and view 3 frames.
10. **Thumbnail** (1280x720): Pillow, dark warm background, big serif name, "BIOGRAPHY FOR SLEEP", subject portrait on the right with soft edge (thumb_franklin.jpg is the template). Canva Pro is available if a polish pass is wanted.
11. **Metadata and delivery.** Title (vidIQ-scored), description (what the video is, AI-narration disclosure, chapter timestamps from timeline.json, image and text sources, "not medical advice"), 15 to 20 tags, first comment text (question to the audience plus a gentle "like and subscribe to stay"), playlist, "Altered or synthetic content = Yes", category Education, not made for kids. Put video.mp4, thumbnail.jpg and metadata.txt in Saurabh's Downloads (franklin-ready style folder), and he uploads in Studio (or hand it to the auto-upload, see Auto-upload below).
12. **Learn and repeat.** 48 hours and 7 days after each upload: read views, average view duration, retention and impressions CTR (YouTube Studio, vidIQ video stats). Keep a Results log in Notion. Copy the patterns of the best video: topic type, title formula, thumbnail layout, chapter length, voice speed. Drop what underperforms.

## Auto-upload (built and tested Nov 9 2026): how a finished video reaches YouTube
The cloud workspace cannot reach YouTube/Google, and GitHub releases, other repos and the file_upload browser tool (10 MB limit) are blocked or too small. What works:
1. `python3 /home/claude/franklin/handover.py <video.mp4> <thumb.jpg> <metadata.txt> <id> <schedule_at_UTC>` splits the video in 90 MB parts, pushes them to a temporary branch `feed-<id>` of the repo Saurabh008malik/smalik-x-poster (about 10 s per 90 MB), adds the item to sleepy/queue.json on main, with the title, description, tags, first comment taken from metadata.txt.
2. Workflow `sleepy-publish.yml` (every 30 min or dispatched with `gh api -X POST repos/Saurabh008malik/smalik-x-poster/actions/workflows/sleepy-publish.yml/dispatches -f ref=main -f 'inputs[action]=publish'`) joins the parts, checks the sha256, asks the Apps Script hook for a 1-hour token (only for The Sleepy Biographer), uploads as PRIVATE with `publishAt` = schedule_at (YouTube makes it public by itself), sets the thumbnail, synthetic-content flag, first comment, then deletes the parts branch.
3. Tested: whoami, token, and a real 5-second unlisted upload that stayed unlisted (so the API project is not locked) and was deleted again (workflow input `uploadtest`).
4. Not possible through the API: pinning the first comment (do it by hand in Studio) and adding to a playlist unless the playlist title matches (the item "playlist" field).
5. Schedule rule from the Notion blueprint: Tue and Fri about 7 pm US Eastern (Nov to Mar = 00:00 UTC next day; summer time = 23:00 UTC). Launch day Tue 17 Nov 2026: Franklin and Leonardo together; Victoria Fri 20 Nov.
Secret: SLEEPY_HOOK (GitHub). If the Apps Script web app is ever redeployed, the URL changes and the secret must be pasted again by Saurabh.

## Known gotchas
- Sandbox network allowlist: only package registries and GitHub; use Chrome for everything else.
- Chrome blocks repeated automatic downloads: build one zip.
- `sleep` over a few seconds is blocked: use Monitor or background until-loops.
- Apps Script editor: set code through `monaco.editor.getModels()[0].setValue(...)`; the manifest appears after ticking "Show appsscript.json" in Project Settings.
- GitHub secrets cannot be set from the sandbox; Saurabh pastes secrets himself.
- GitHub releases, new repos, branch deletion from the sandbox and large file_upload calls are blocked; deleting branches works only inside the Action (GITHUB_TOKEN).
- Ubuntu runner has no ffmpeg by default (apt-get install it where needed).
- WebFetch/shell cannot reach Google endpoints; only the Action and Saurabh's Chrome can.
