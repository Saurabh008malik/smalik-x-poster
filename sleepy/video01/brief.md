# Benjamin Franklin: research brief (video 1)

Working title: Benjamin Franklin: The Printer Who Lit Up the World | Biography for Sleep
Length target: about 90 minutes (about 12,500 words at about 140 wpm)

## Timeline and chapters (12)

1. Welcome and the boy from Milk Street: birth 17 Jan 1706 (Old Style), Boston; father Josiah, a soap and candle maker from Ecton, England; Franklin the youngest son among Josiah's 17 children by two wives; the harbour town.
2. School and books: one year at Boston Latin, then a writing school; leaves school at about 10; works in the candle shop; loves reading, loves the sea; at 12 is apprenticed to his brother James, a printer (1718).
3. The apprentice and Silence Dogood: James starts the New-England Courant (1721); Franklin, aged 16, writes fourteen letters as the fictional widow Silence Dogood (1722), slipped under the door; learns to write by rewriting The Spectator; vegetarian phase.
4. The runaway: breaks with James; leaves Boston in autumn 1723; New York; walks and boats to Philadelphia; the three rolls; sees Deborah Read.
5. London: 1724-26, promised letters from Governor Keith never arrive; works as a printer in London; returns Oct 1726 with merchant Thomas Denham; Denham's death 1728.
6. Building a life in Philadelphia: Junto (1727), printing shop, buys the Pennsylvania Gazette (1729), marries Deborah (1 Sept 1730), the Library Company (1731), thirteen virtues, Poor Richard's Almanack (from Dec 1732).
7. Home and loss: William, Francis Folger (born 1732, died of smallpox 1736, aged 4), Sally; postmaster 1737; fire company 1736; the Franklin stove (1740s); retires from printing at 42 (1748).
8. The scientist: electricity from 1746; the kite, June 1752 (the Franklin Institute account); lightning rod; the danger (Richmann 1753); Copley Medal 1753; honorary degrees; Gulf Stream chart; glass armonica 1761; bifocals.
9. The civic builder: American Philosophical Society (1743), Academy (1749, now Penn), Pennsylvania Hospital (1751), paving and lighting; Albany Plan and "Join or Die" (1754).
10. London years: agent for Pennsylvania from 1757; again from 1764; Stamp Act and House of Commons examination (Feb 1766); the Cockpit, 29 Jan 1774; Deborah dies 19 Dec 1774; he leaves in March 1775; William the Loyalist.
11. The Revolution and Paris: arrives in Philadelphia 5 May 1775; the Declaration committee; sails Oct 1776; Passy; the treaties of 6 Feb 1778; Treaty of Paris, 3 Sept 1783; the plain fur cap.
12. Last years: home Sept 1785; Constitutional Convention 1787, age 81, the rising sun; Pennsylvania Abolition Society; petition to Congress, Feb 1790; death 17 April 1790; funeral 21 April, at least 20,000 people; epitaph of 1728; goodnight.

## Facts to treat with care (told as uncertain or avoided)

- Franklin's birthday: 17 Jan 1706 is Old Style; we say "January 17th, 1706, by the calendar of the time".
- Kite: Franklin's own 1752 account and Priestley's later account; the kite was probably NOT struck by lightning; we say so.
- Franklin did not "discover electricity" and did not invent the lightning rod idea alone from the kite; he proposed the rod from his experiments.
- Daylight saving: his 1784 essay was a humorous letter, so we mention it only as a joke, or not at all.
- Scenes of weather, smells and light are described as imagined ("you might picture") because no source records them.
- No invented quotes: only lines from his own writings (public domain), short.
