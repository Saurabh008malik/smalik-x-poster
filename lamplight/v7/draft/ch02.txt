## Chapter 2: A Silent Priest and a Hidden Joy

We left Zacharias standing in the holy place, beside the golden altar, with the angel's words still in the air. A son, after all these years. A son named John, who would prepare the way.

And Zacharias does what so many of us would do. He asks how he can be sure.

{{LUK 1:18-20}}

It is a very human question. How can I be sure of this? I am an old man, and my wife is well advanced in years. Zacharias had lived long enough to know how the world works. Old women do not have babies. Prayers from long ago do not suddenly come true in an afternoon.

And the angel gives his name. I am Gabriel, he says, who stands in the presence of God. Gabriel is one of only two angels named anywhere in the old Hebrew scriptures, both times in the book of Daniel, where he comes to explain visions to a man praying far away in Babylon. Now, centuries later, he has come again, this time into the temple itself.

The sign Zacharias asked for is given, though perhaps not the kind of sign he wanted. He will be silent. He will not be able to speak until the day these things happen. It is often read as a rebuke for his doubt, and the angel does say so. But there is something gentle in it as well. For the next many months, the old priest will not be able to explain away what he has seen, or talk himself out of it. He will simply have to wait, and watch, and keep it in his heart.

Meanwhile, outside, the people are waiting.

{{LUK 1:21-23}}

Let us picture the crowd in the temple courts. The incense offering usually took only a short time. But Zacharias stays inside, and stays inside, and the people begin to wonder. Has something gone wrong? Has he fallen ill, alone in the holy place?

And then at last he comes out. And instead of lifting his hands and speaking the blessing over them, as they expected, he stands there making signs with his hands, unable to say a word. And slowly the people understand that something has happened in there. He has seen a vision.

There is something lovely in that picture. An old man standing in the morning light at the top of the temple steps, his face perhaps still shining, waving his hands, trying to tell them without words. And all the people, puzzled, quieted, wondering.

Zacharias finishes his week of service. He does not run home early. He stays and does his duty, silent, until the days are done. And then he goes back to his own house, in a town in the hill country of Judea. Luke does not tell us which town. Later Christian tradition remembered it as Ein Karem, a village in the green hills just to the west of Jerusalem, but Luke himself leaves it unnamed.

And then, quietly, the promise begins to come true.

{{LUK 1:24-25}}

Elizabeth conceives. And she does something interesting. She hides herself for five months. Luke does not explain why. Perhaps she wanted to be certain first, after so many years of disappointment. Perhaps she simply wanted to hold this joy close, to herself, for a while, before the whole town began to talk about it. Some joys are too tender to share right away.

And she says a beautiful thing. The Lord has done this for me. He has looked at me, to take away my reproach among people.

Think of all the years she had spent with that reproach. All the times she had seen the other women of the village with their babies on their hips. All the kind and not so kind questions. All the gatherings where she sat a little apart. And now, in her old age, the Lord has looked at her. That is how she puts it. He has looked at me. As if, all those years, she had felt unseen, and now, at last, she is seen.

So for five months, in a house in the hill country, an old woman keeps her secret. And her husband, who cannot speak, keeps it with her. The house must have been very quiet. Two old people, sitting by the lamp in the evenings, one of them unable to say a word, the other choosing not to say it to anyone else yet. A quiet house, full of a joy that was growing slowly, out of sight.

Let us leave them there, for now, in that quiet house in the hills. Because Luke is about to take us on a journey, far to the north, several days' walk by the roads of that time, over hills and valleys, to a small village in Galilee, where a young woman has no idea that her life is about to change.
