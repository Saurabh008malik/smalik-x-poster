## Chapter 9: Shepherds Keeping Watch

Out beyond the edge of Bethlehem, the land falls away in long, rough slopes and terraces, toward the wilderness in the east. It is shepherds' country. It has been shepherds' country for a thousand years, ever since a boy named David watched his father's sheep there.

And on this night, Luke tells us, there were shepherds out in the fields.

{{LUK 2:8}}

Living out in the field, and keeping watch by night over their flock. Let us picture them, gently. A few men, perhaps some boys among them, wrapped in heavy cloaks of wool or goat hair. A small fire, maybe, burning low. The sheep huddled together in a rough pen of stones, or lying on the hillside nearby. The long hours of the night watch, when one man sleeps and another stays awake, listening for the sound of a wolf or a thief.

Shepherds were not important people. Their work was hard, and dirty, and it kept them out in the fields, away from the towns, often unable to keep the careful customs of the religious leaders. Some later Jewish writings even list shepherds among trades that were looked down on. They were the sort of people who were easy to overlook.

Which is, perhaps, exactly why they were chosen.

{{LUK 2:9-12}}

Suddenly, in the dark, there is light. The glory of the Lord shines around them. And the shepherds are terrified. That is the word the old text uses. Terrified.

And then come the words, one more time. Don't be afraid.

We have heard them now, again and again, all through this story. To Zacharias in the sanctuary. To Mary in her house. To Joseph in his dream. And now to a few frightened shepherds on a cold hillside. Don't be afraid. It is almost as if this is the first thing heaven always wants to say.

For behold, I bring you good news of great joy, which will be to all the people.

Good news. Earlier we heard that some of the emperor's subjects called him a bringer of good news. Now an angel brings good news to shepherds, and it is not about an emperor. It is about a baby.

For there is born to you today, in David's city, a Savior, who is Christ the Lord.

To you, the angel says. Not to the priests. Not to the king in Jerusalem. To you. To shepherds.

And then the angel gives them a sign. And it is the strangest sign. Not a star, or a thunderclap, or a voice from the clouds. This is the sign to you. You will find a baby wrapped in strips of cloth, lying in a feeding trough.

That is how they will know. A baby, in a manger. The most ordinary thing, in the most humble place. Shepherds knew all about mangers. They could find one.

And then, before they can even begin to take it in, the whole sky opens.

{{LUK 2:13-14}}

A multitude of the heavenly army. That is what the old text calls them. An army. But this army does not come with swords. It comes singing. Praising God.

Glory to God in the highest, on earth peace, good will toward men.

Those words have been sung in more languages, and set to more music, than almost any others. In great cathedrals with organs and choirs. In small chapels with one guitar. By children in school halls, in paper crowns and tinsel. But the first time, the very first time, they were sung to a few tired shepherds, out in a field in the dark.

Peace on earth. In a land ruled by a suspicious king, under the shadow of Roman soldiers. Peace, sung over the hills of Bethlehem, in the middle of the night.

And then, just as suddenly, it is over.

{{LUK 2:15-16}}

The angels go away into the sky. The light fades. The dark comes back, and the stars, and the cold, and the sheep. And the shepherds look at one another.

And they do not argue. They do not wonder whether they imagined it. They say, let's go. Let's go to Bethlehem, and see this thing that has happened.

They came with haste, Luke says. Imagine them hurrying down the slopes in the dark, cloaks flapping, stumbling on the stones, laughing perhaps, or breathless with wonder. Into the sleeping town. Looking for a light in a doorway. Asking, perhaps, at one house and then another, has a baby been born here tonight?

And then they find them. Mary, and Joseph, and the baby lying in the feeding trough. Just as they had been told.

{{LUK 2:17-18}}

The shepherds tell everyone. Mary and Joseph first, surely, and then anyone else who will listen. The angels. The light. The song. They tell it all, and everyone who hears it wonders.

And then Luke gives us one of the most beautiful sentences in the whole story.

{{LUK 2:19}}

But Mary kept all these sayings, pondering them in her heart.

While everyone else is talking, and wondering, and telling the story again, Mary is quiet. She keeps it all. She turns it over, slowly, in her heart. The angel in Nazareth. Elizabeth's cry of joy. The long road. The manger. And now these rough men from the fields, with their story of angels singing in the sky.

Luke says this about her more than once. She keeps things. She ponders. Perhaps that is how Luke knew these stories at all. Perhaps, many years later, an old woman told them to someone, quietly, remembering.

{{LUK 2:20}}

And the shepherds go back. Back to the fields, and the sheep, and the cold, and the long night watch. Nothing about their work has changed. Tomorrow will be just as hard as yesterday. But they go back glorifying and praising God, for all the things they had heard and seen.

Let us leave them there, on the hillside, under the stars. The fire burning low again. The sheep settling down. A few shepherds, sitting close together, talking quietly in the dark, about the night the sky was filled with singing.
