## Chapter 11: The Golden Scepter, and a Night Without Sleep

{{EST 5:1-8}}

On the third day, Esther put on her royal clothing and stood in the inner court of the king's house, across from the king's house. And the king was sitting on his royal throne, facing the entrance.

Imagine the silence of that moment. The long, high room. The light coming in from the court. The king on his throne at the far end. And the queen standing there, uninvited, waiting to see what he will do.

And when the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, she found favor in his sight. And the king held out to Esther the golden scepter that was in his hand. So Esther came near, and touched the top of the scepter.

What is it, Queen Esther, the king asks. What is your request? It will be given to you, even to half of the kingdom.

And here Esther does something very wise. She does not blurt out her request. She does not accuse anyone. She simply invites the king, and Haman with him, to a banquet she has prepared. And at the banquet, when the king asks again what she wants, she invites them both to another banquet the next day. Tomorrow, she says, I will do as the king has said.

Perhaps she was waiting for the right moment. Perhaps she was gathering her courage. Or perhaps, as the story is about to show us, the right moment had not yet come, and something needed to happen first, in the night.

{{EST 5:9-14}}

Haman goes home that day joyful and glad of heart. Two banquets with the king and queen. No one else invited. But on the way, he passes Mordecai in the king's gate, and Mordecai does not stand up, or move, and Haman is filled with fury again.

He goes home and gathers his friends and his wife Zeresh, and boasts to them of all his riches and honors. And then he says, Yet all this avails me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate.

So his wife and his friends suggest that he have a gallows made, fifty cubits high, very tall, and in the morning ask the king to have Mordecai hanged on it, and then go merrily to the banquet. And the idea pleases Haman, and he has the gallows built.

And now comes the night. And what a night it is.

{{EST 6:1-3}}

On that night, the king could not sleep.

That is all the old story says. It gives no reason. Perhaps he had eaten too much at the banquet. Perhaps he was wondering what Esther wanted. Perhaps it was just one of those nights that everyone has, when sleep will not come. Many listeners of this channel will know what that is like.

So the king commands that the book of records, the chronicles, be brought and read to him. Perhaps he hoped it would be dull enough to send him to sleep. And a servant takes down the scrolls, and begins to read, in the lamplight. And out of all the years of records in the royal archive, the reading comes to the place where it was written that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, the two chamberlains who had plotted to kill the king.

The book that had been put away on the shelf, years ago, has been taken down.

What honor and dignity has been given to Mordecai for this, the king asks. And his servants answer, Nothing has been done for him.

{{EST 6:4-11}}

And just at that moment, in the early hours of the morning, Haman arrives in the outer court of the king's house, to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he has built.

The king hears someone in the court, and asks who is there, and they tell him it is Haman. Let him come in. And before Haman can say a word about Mordecai, the king asks him a question. What should be done to the man whom the king delights to honor?

And Haman thinks in his heart, Whom would the king delight to honor more than me? So he describes, in loving detail, the honor he would like for himself. Let royal clothing be brought, that the king has worn, and the horse that the king has ridden, with a royal crown on its head. Let one of the king's most noble princes dress the man, and lead him on horseback through the city square, and proclaim before him, This is what is done to the man whom the king delights to honor.

Then the king says to Haman, Hurry, and take the clothing and the horse, as you have said, and do this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king's gate. Let nothing fail of all that you have spoken.

The old storyteller does not laugh out loud, but you can almost hear him smiling. So Haman takes the robe and the horse, and dresses Mordecai, and leads him through the streets of Susa, calling out before him, This is what is done to the man whom the king delights to honor.

All because, one night, a king could not sleep.

{{EST 6:12-14}}

Afterward, Mordecai goes back to the king's gate, as quietly as before. But Haman hurries home, mourning, with his head covered. And even his wife and his wise friends now tell him that things have turned. And while they are still talking with him, the king's chamberlains arrive, and hurry Haman away to the banquet that Esther has prepared.
