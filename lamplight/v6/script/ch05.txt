## Chapter 5: A Night on the Threshing Floor

One day, near the end of the harvest, Naomi spoke to Ruth about the future. She had been thinking about it, perhaps for many nights. Ruth was young. She had given up her own country for Naomi's sake. Naomi wanted her to have a home of her own, and rest, and a husband who would care for her.

Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, My daughter, shall I not seek rest for you, that it may be well with you? Now isn't Boaz our kinsman, with whose maidens you were? Behold, he will be winnowing barley tonight on the threshing floor. Therefore wash yourself, anoint yourself, get dressed, and go down to the threshing floor; but don't make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking. It shall be, when he lies down, that you shall note the place where he is lying. Then you shall go in, uncover his feet, and lie down. Then he will tell you what to do. She said to her, All that you say, I will do.

To our ears, this plan may sound strange, and it is worth explaining it gently. Naomi is telling Ruth to go and make a quiet, respectful proposal. Boaz is a kinsman, a redeemer. By the customs of Israel, he has the right to marry the widow of his relative and so carry on the family name and keep the family land. But he is an older man, and Ruth is a young foreigner, and perhaps he has not thought it would be proper to ask. So Naomi tells Ruth to go to him herself, privately, at night, when no one else is around, and to lie down at his feet, and let him know what she is asking. It is a delicate, trusting thing to do. And Ruth answers simply. All that you say, I will do.

She went down to the threshing floor, and did everything that her mother-in-law told her. When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. She came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down. At midnight, the man was startled and turned himself; and behold, a woman lay at his feet. He said, Who are you? She answered, I am Ruth your servant. Therefore spread the corner of your garment over your servant; for you are a near kinsman.

Picture it. The winnowing is over for the day. The workers have eaten and gone to sleep. The heaps of grain lie dark against the starlight. Boaz has lain down at the end of his heap, contented, and fallen asleep. The night breeze moves softly over the hill.

And Ruth comes very quietly, and uncovers his feet, and lies down.

At midnight, Boaz wakes with a start. The night has grown cold, perhaps, and he turns over, and there is someone lying at his feet. Who are you, he asks.

I am Ruth, she says, your servant. Spread the corner of your garment over your servant, for you are a near kinsman.

Do you remember the picture from the barley field? Boaz had blessed her in the name of the Lord, under whose wings you have come to take refuge. Now Ruth uses the same word. In Hebrew, the corner of a garment and the wing of a bird are the same word, kanaph. Spread your wing over me, she says. You prayed that I would find shelter under the wings of God. Now be that shelter yourself.

He said, You are blessed by the Lord, my daughter. You have shown more kindness in the latter end than at the beginning, because you didn't follow young men, whether poor or rich. Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I will do to you all that you say; for all the city of my people knows that you are a worthy woman. Now it is true that I am a near kinsman. However, there is a kinsman nearer than I. Stay this night, and in the morning, if he will perform for you the part of a kinsman, good. Let him do the kinsman's duty. But if he will not do the duty of a kinsman for you, then I will do the duty of a kinsman for you, as the Lord lives. Lie down until the morning.

Boaz is moved. You have shown even more kindness now than at the first, he says, because you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich. He means that she could have looked for a husband for herself alone. Instead she has come to the kinsman, so that the name of Naomi's family will live on. Again it is hesed, loyal love, for Naomi's sake.

Don't be afraid, he tells her. I will do for you all that you say, for all the town knows that you are a worthy woman. In Hebrew it is the same word that was used of Boaz himself at the beginning, when he was called a man of worth. Here are two people of worth, meeting in the dark on a threshing floor.

But there is one complication, and Boaz is an honest man, so he tells her about it. There is another relative, he says, who is nearer than I am. He has the first right. If he wants to redeem, then let him. But if he does not, then as the Lord lives, I will. Lie down until the morning.

She lay at his feet until the morning, then she rose up before one could discern another. For he said, Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor. He said, Bring the mantle that is on you, and hold it. She held it; and he measured six measures of barley, and laid it on her; then he went into the city. When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, How did it go, my daughter? She told her all that the man had done for her. She said, He gave me these six measures of barley; for he said, Don't go empty to your mother-in-law. Then she said, Wait, my daughter, until you know what will happen; for the man will not rest until he has settled this today.

So Ruth lay at his feet until the morning, and she rose before anyone could recognize another person in the gray light. And Boaz, before she went, told her to hold out her shawl, and he poured six measures of barley into it, and lifted it onto her. He did not want her to go back to her mother-in-law empty.

Empty. Do you remember? When Naomi came home, she said, I went out full, and the Lord brought me home empty. And now here comes Ruth, through the dawn, with her shawl heavy with barley, carrying a message that says, in its own quiet way, you will not be empty anymore.

When Ruth told her everything, Naomi said, Wait, my daughter, until you know how the matter turns out. The man will not rest until he has settled this today.

So let us wait with them, for a little while, in that small house in Bethlehem. The morning light is coming in at the door. The barley is in a heap on the floor. Two women are sitting together, very quietly, listening for footsteps in the street.
