## Chapter 5: Pharaoh's Dreams

At the end of two full years, Pharaoh dreamed.

He was standing by the river, the great river, the Nile, which was the whole life of Egypt. And out of the river came seven cows, sleek and fat, and they fed in the reed grass. And behind them came seven other cows, ugly and thin, and they stood beside the fat cows on the bank. And the ugly, thin cows ate up the seven sleek, fat cows. And Pharaoh woke.

He slept again, and dreamed a second time. Seven heads of grain came up on one stalk, healthy and good. And behind them sprang up seven heads, thin and blasted by the east wind. And the thin heads swallowed up the seven healthy, full heads. And Pharaoh woke, and behold, it was a dream.

In the morning his spirit was troubled. He sent and called for all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men, and he told them his dreams, and there was no one who could interpret them to Pharaoh.

Picture the court. The most learned men in the most learned kingdom on earth, standing in rows, and not one of them able to say what seven thin cows eating seven fat cows might mean. And somewhere along the wall, holding a cup, the chief cupbearer, feeling something stir in his memory.

Then the chief cupbearer spoke to Pharaoh. I remember my faults today, he said. Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and put me in custody in the house of the captain of the guard, me and the chief baker. We dreamed a dream in one night, he and I. There was with us there a young man, a Hebrew, servant to the captain of the guard, and we told him, and he interpreted to us our dreams. To each man according to his dream he interpreted. And it happened, as he interpreted to us, so it was. Pharaoh restored me to my office, and he hanged him.

A young man, a Hebrew. The cupbearer does not even remember his name. But it was enough.

Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they brought him hastily out of the dungeon. And he shaved himself, and changed his clothing, and came in to Pharaoh. Egyptians were clean-shaven; a Hebrew shepherd was not. So Joseph, in the space of an hour, was made to look like an Egyptian courtier, and brought from the lowest place in the kingdom to the highest. The clothes again. For the third time in his life, Joseph's clothing is changed by someone else's hand. But this time it is a change upward.

Here are the old words.

Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I have dreamed a dream, and there is no one who can interpret it. I have heard it said of you, that when you hear a dream you can interpret it." Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, "It isn't in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace."

It is not in me. He is standing before the most powerful man in the world, who has just handed him the chance of a lifetime, and the first words out of his mouth are, it is not in me. He gives the credit away before he has earned it. That is the same man who said in the prison, do not interpretations belong to God. The years have not made him bitter. They have made him clear.

Pharaoh told him the dreams, the cows and the grain, in more detail than before, and you can hear in the retelling how much they had frightened him. The thin cows, he says, were such as I never saw in all the land of Egypt for ugliness. And when they had eaten the fat cows up, it could not be known that they had eaten them. They were still as ugly as at the beginning.

And Joseph said, the dream of Pharaoh is one. God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do. The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads of grain are seven years. The dream is one. The seven thin and ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and also the seven empty heads of grain blasted with the east wind. They will be seven years of famine. Behold, seven years of great plenty are coming throughout all the land of Egypt. And after them will arise seven years of famine, and all the plenty will be forgotten, and the famine will consume the land. And the dream was doubled to Pharaoh, because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.

And then Joseph did something no one had asked him to do. He went on. He did not stop at the interpretation. He offered a plan. Now therefore, he said, let Pharaoh look for a discreet and wise man, and set him over the land of Egypt. Let Pharaoh appoint overseers, and take up a fifth part of the land's produce in the seven plenteous years. Let them gather all the food of these good years, and store grain under the hand of Pharaoh in the cities, and let them keep it. The food will be for a store against the seven years of famine, so that the land will not perish through the famine.

Seven years of abundance, and a fifth of every harvest put away. A grain tax, in plain terms, and a national store. It is not a miracle. It is management. Joseph had spent thirteen years running other people's households and prisons, and he knew exactly how a thing like this would need to be done.

Here are the old words.

Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find such a one as this, a man in whom is the Spirit of God?" Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Because God has shown you all of this, there is none so discreet and wise as you. You shall be over my house, and according to your word will all my people be ruled. Only in the throne I will be greater than you." Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Behold, I have set you over all the land of Egypt."

And Pharaoh took off his signet ring from his hand, and put it on Joseph's hand. He dressed him in robes of fine linen, and put a gold chain around his neck, and had him ride in the second chariot which he had, and they cried before him, bow the knee. He gave him an Egyptian name, Zaphenath-Paneah, and gave him a wife, Asenath, the daughter of a priest of On. And Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh, king of Egypt.

Thirty. He had been seventeen in the pit. Thirteen years, from the coat of many colors to the robes of fine linen, by way of a slave market, a rich man's house, a false accusation, and a prison. And now the dreams of the sheaves, which his brothers had thrown into a hole in the ground, were standing up in the middle of Egypt in a gold chain.

That is where we will rest tonight. On the morning Joseph came out of the dungeon. Shaved, washed, dressed, lifted up, and set over everything. The seven years of plenty are about to begin, and there is going to be more grain than anyone can count.

Let it be a good morning, for a while. He had earned one.
