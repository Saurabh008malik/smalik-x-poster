## Chapter 8: Is Your Father Well?

When Joseph saw Benjamin with them, he said to the steward of his house, bring the men into the house, and butcher an animal and make ready, for the men will dine with me at noon.

The brothers were terrified. They were led, not to the granary, but to the governor's own house, and they could only imagine one reason for it. Because of the money that was returned in our sacks the first time, they said to each other, we are brought in, so that he may seek occasion against us, and fall on us, and take us for slaves, with our donkeys. So they stopped the steward at the door and tried to explain about the silver, all of it, in a rush. We came down the first time to buy food, and when we opened our sacks, behold, every man's money was there, full weight, and we have brought it back with us, and other money too. We do not know who put it there.

And the steward said something that must have stopped them where they stood. Peace be to you. Do not be afraid. Your God, and the God of your father, has given you treasure in your sacks. I received your money.

Peace be to you. An Egyptian steward, blessing them in the name of the God of their father. And then he brought Simeon out to them, and brought them into the house, and gave them water, and they washed their feet, and he fed their donkeys. The small kindnesses of a host. They got the present ready for when Joseph would come at noon, because they had heard they would eat bread there.

When Joseph came home, they brought him the present, and bowed themselves down to him to the earth. Bowing again. The sheaves. And Joseph asked them about their welfare, and then he asked the question he had been carrying for twenty-two years, and he asked it as casually as he could manage.

Is your father well, the old man of whom you spoke? Is he yet alive?

Your servant our father is well, they said. He is still alive. And they bowed their heads.

And then Joseph looked past them.

Here are the old words.

He lifted up his eyes, and saw Benjamin, his brother, his mother's son, and said, "Is this your youngest brother, of whom you spoke to me?" He said, "God be gracious to you, my son." Joseph hurried, for his heart yearned over his brother; and he sought a place to weep. He entered into his room, and wept there.

His mother's son. The text does not need to say it. It says it anyway. Of all these eleven men, this was the only one who had shared Rachel. Joseph had last seen him as a small child. Now he was a grown man, standing in the governor's hall, bowing, with no idea who was looking at him. God be gracious to you, my son. And then Joseph could not hold it. He hurried out. He found a room. He wept there.

Then he washed his face, and came out, and controlled himself, and said, serve the meal.

And they served him by himself, and the brothers by themselves, and the Egyptians who ate with him by themselves, because the Egyptians would not eat with Hebrews. Three tables in one room. And then a strange thing happened that the brothers could not explain. They were seated before him, the firstborn according to his birthright, and the youngest according to his youth. In order. Oldest to youngest, eleven men, in exactly the right order, seated by a foreign governor who could not possibly have known which of them was born when. And the men marveled with one another.

Joseph sent portions to them from his own table. But Benjamin's portion was five times as much as any of theirs. And they drank, and were merry with him.

Five times as much. It is a test, of course, like everything Joseph does in these chapters. He is watching. Twenty-two years ago, a father's open favoritism toward one of Rachel's sons had made ten brothers hate him. Now the governor is showing open favoritism toward the other of Rachel's sons, in front of the same ten men. Will they resent it? Will they look at Benjamin's heaped plate the way they looked at the coat of many colors? And the text says only, they drank, and were merry with him. No bitterness. Not yet, at least. Joseph is beginning to hope.

But he has one more test, and it is the hardest one.

He commanded the steward of his house, saying, fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man's money in the mouth of his sack. And put my cup, the silver cup, in the mouth of the sack of the youngest, with his grain money. And the steward did so.

In the morning, as soon as it was light, the men were sent away, they and their donkeys. And when they had gone out of the city, and were not yet far off, Joseph said to his steward, up, follow after the men. And when you overtake them, say to them, why have you rewarded evil for good? Is not this the cup my lord drinks from? You have done evil in doing this.

The steward caught up with them and said those words. And the brothers answered, in full innocence, why does my lord speak such words as these? Far be it from your servants that they should do such a thing. Behold, the money which we found in our sacks' mouths, we brought back to you out of the land of Canaan. How then should we steal silver or gold out of your lord's house? With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die, and we also will be my lord's slaves.

Let him die. They had no idea. They were so certain, so outraged, that they offered the harshest possible penalty. And the steward said, let it be as you say. He with whom it is found will be my slave, and the rest of you will be blameless.

And they hurried, and every man took his sack down to the ground, and every man opened his sack. And the steward searched. He began with the eldest and finished with the youngest. Sack after sack. Reuben, nothing. Simeon, nothing. Levi, nothing. Judah, nothing. Down the line, ten sacks, ten nothings, and the brothers starting to breathe again.

And the cup was found in Benjamin's sack.

Then they tore their clothes. All of them. The text does not say Benjamin tore his clothes. It says they tore their clothes, every man, and loaded his donkey, and returned to the city.

That is the moment. Do you see it? They could have gone home. The steward had said it himself. The rest of you will be blameless. The guilty one would stay, the rest were free, and it was Rachel's son again, the favorite again, the one with the five portions and the father's whole heart. Twenty-two years ago, ten men had walked away from Rachel's son in a pit and gone home with a story.

This time, all eleven turned around and went back.

Rest here a moment, on the road outside the city, with the sacks open on the ground and the silver cup shining in the sun and ten men choosing, without a word, not to leave their brother. The next chapter is Judah's. It is the finest speech in the book of Genesis. And it is coming, but there is no hurry.
