## Chapter 9: Judah Steps Forward

Judah and his brothers came to Joseph's house, and he was still there, and they fell on the ground before him. And Joseph said to them, what deed is this that you have done? Do you not know that such a man as I can indeed divine?

And Judah said, what will we tell my lord? What will we speak? How will we clear ourselves? God has found out the iniquity of your servants. Behold, we are my lord's slaves, both we and he also in whose hand the cup is found.

God has found out the iniquity of your servants. He is not talking about the cup. He knows they did not take the cup. He is talking about something older. He is saying, out loud, in front of the Egyptian lord, that whatever is happening to them now, they had it coming, for a reason this man could not possibly know.

And Joseph gave them the way out one last time. Far be it from me that I should do so, he said. The man in whose hand the cup is found, he will be my slave. But as for you, go up in peace to your father.

Go up in peace to your father. Leave the boy. Go home. It is the pit all over again, offered openly, with permission. And Judah came near to him.

I am going to tell you Judah's speech in my own words, slowly, because it is long and it is the heart of the whole story. It is the longest speech any human being makes in the book of Genesis. And it is made by the man who once said, let us sell him.

Oh my lord, Judah said, please let your servant speak a word in my lord's ears, and do not let your anger burn against your servant, for you are even as Pharaoh. My lord asked his servants, saying, have you a father, or a brother? And we said to my lord, we have a father, an old man, and a child of his old age, a little one, and his brother is dead, and he alone is left of his mother, and his father loves him.

And you said to your servants, bring him down to me, that I may set my eyes on him. We said to my lord, the boy cannot leave his father, for if he should leave his father, his father would die. And you said to your servants, unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you will see my face no more.

When we came up to your servant my father, we told him the words of my lord. And our father said, go again, buy us a little food. And we said, we cannot go down. If our youngest brother is with us, then we will go down, for we may not see the man's face unless our youngest brother is with us.

And your servant my father said to us, you know that my wife bore me two sons. And the one went out from me, and I said, surely he is torn in pieces, and I have not seen him since. And if you take this one also from me, and harm happens to him, you will bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave.

Listen to what Judah is doing. He is standing in front of the man he sold, and he does not know it, and he is quoting their father. My wife bore me two sons. Jacob had twelve sons, and he calls Rachel his wife and her boys his two sons, in front of the other ten, and Judah repeats it without complaint. He has made peace with being second. He is only trying to save his father's heart.

Now therefore, Judah went on, when I come to your servant my father, and the boy is not with us, since his life is bound up in the boy's life, it will happen, when he sees that the boy is not with us, that he will die. And your servants will bring down the gray hairs of your servant our father with sorrow to the grave. For your servant became collateral for the boy to my father, saying, if I do not bring him to you, then I will bear the blame to my father forever.

And then the last lines, which I will read in the old words.

Now therefore, please let your servant stay instead of the boy, a bondservant to my lord; and let the boy go up with his brothers. For how will I go up to my father, if the boy isn't with me?-- lest I see the evil that will come on my father.

Let your servant stay instead of the boy.

Instead of. Judah, who sold one of Rachel's sons into slavery, is offering to become a slave in place of the other. He is not pleading innocence. He is not arguing about the cup. He is saying, take me. Let the favorite go home to his father. I will stay in Egypt forever, so that an old man I have already hurt does not have to be hurt again.

This is what Joseph had been waiting to find out. Not whether his brothers were sorry. Sorry is cheap, and he had already heard them say they were guilty. He needed to know whether they had changed. Whether, given the exact same choice they had been given twenty-two years ago, with Rachel's son in trouble and a clear road home, they would do the same thing again.

And Judah stepped forward and answered the question with his own life.

The old teachers noticed something about this. The man who offers to be a substitute, to stand in the place of someone condemned so that they can go free, is Judah. And the line of Judah, in the long story of the Bible, is the line of kings. Something begins here, in this speech, in this house in Egypt, that the rest of scripture will keep returning to. A person standing in another person's place.

Joseph could not hold it any longer. We will hear what he does in the next chapter. For now, let the room be silent. Eleven men on their faces. An Egyptian governor, trembling, trying to keep his face still. And the echo of a sentence that would have been unimaginable twenty-two years ago, hanging in the air. Please let your servant stay instead of the boy.

Sleep on that, if you can. It is one of the best things a human being ever said.
