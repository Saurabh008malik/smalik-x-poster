## Chapter 12: By the Great River, and Goodnight

The last vision in the book of Daniel comes in the third year of Cyrus king of Persia. Daniel has lived to see Babylon fall, and the first exiles set out for home. He has outlived Nebuchadnezzar, and Belshazzar, and all the officials who once plotted against him. If the years of the book are counted simply, he must now be well into his eighties.

The book tells us that for three whole weeks before this vision, Daniel had been mourning. He ate no rich food, no meat or wine, and he did not anoint himself with oil. We are not told exactly why. Perhaps he grieved for the troubles of his people, or for the long road still ahead of them. And then, on the twenty-fourth day of the first month, in the spring, he was standing beside a river.

In the twenty-fourth day of the first month, as I was by the side of the great river, which is Hiddekel, I lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, there was a man clothed in linen, whose waist was adorned with pure gold of Uphaz.

The great river, which is Hiddekel. That is the old Hebrew name for the Tigris, the other great river of that land, flowing down out of the northern mountains, wide and brown and strong in the spring. We can picture the old man standing on its bank, perhaps with a few companions, the reeds moving in the wind, the water sliding past. And then he looks up, and sees a man clothed in linen, shining, with a voice like the sound of a great crowd.

The book says that Daniel's strength left him, and he fell on his face to the ground. And then a hand touched him, gently, and raised him to his knees, and a voice spoke to him. And, as so often in this book, the first words were words of comfort.

Then one like the appearance of a man touched me again, and he strengthened me. He said, Greatly beloved man, don't be afraid. Peace be to you. Be strong. Yes, be strong. When he spoke to me, I was strengthened, and said, Let my lord speak, for you have strengthened me.

Greatly beloved man, don't be afraid. Peace be to you. Be strong. Yes, be strong.

If you take only one thing from this whole long story tonight, let it be those words. They were spoken to an old man, tired and trembling, beside a river, far from home. Do not be afraid. Peace be to you. Be strong.

The messenger then spoke to Daniel for a long time, of kings of the north and kings of the south, of wars and troubles and times to come. Those chapters are among the most difficult in all of scripture, and we will let them rest. But near the very end, there is a promise, and it is full of light.

Those who are wise will shine as the brightness of the expanse. Those who turn many to righteousness will shine as the stars forever and ever.

Those who are wise will shine like the brightness of the sky, and those who turn many to righteousness will shine like the stars forever and ever.

Think of the night sky over Babylon, the same sky that its astronomers watched and recorded on their clay tablets for hundreds of years. The same stars that Daniel would have seen from his rooftop, every clear night of his long life. The book says that the wise, the faithful, the ones who quietly help others toward what is good, will shine like that. Not like a golden statue on a plain, or a king's name stamped on a brick, but like the stars, steady and quiet and far beyond the reach of any empire.

And then Daniel asked one more question. He did not understand everything he had seen, and he was honest about it.

I heard, but I didn't understand. Then I said, My lord, what will be the outcome of these things? He said, Go your way, Daniel; for the words are shut up and sealed until the time of the end.

I heard, but I didn't understand. Even Daniel, the wisest man in Babylon, the one who read the king's dreams and the writing on the wall, came to the end of his life with questions he could not answer. And he was told, gently, that it was all right. Go your way, Daniel.

And the book ends with one last sentence, spoken to the old man by the river. It is one of the most peaceful endings in the whole Bible.

But go your way until the end; for you will rest, and will stand in your inheritance at the end of the days.

Go your way until the end, for you will rest.

Let us look back, slowly, over the long road we have walked tonight. A small city in the hills, and a long journey east. Four young men at a foreign king's table, with their bowls of vegetables and their cups of water. A king awake in the night, and a young man praying with his friends by lamplight. A great statue of gold and silver and bronze and iron and clay, and a small stone that became a mountain. A golden image on a wide plain, and three friends who would not bow, and a fourth figure walking with them in the fire. A great tree cut down, and a stump left in the dew, and a proud king who looked up to heaven and found his mind again. A hand writing on the wall beside a lamp. An open window toward Jerusalem. A long night among the lions, and an old man's calm voice in the morning. And at the end, a quiet river, and a voice saying, peace be to you.

Through all of it, Daniel did not do anything very dramatic. He did not lead armies. He did not overthrow kings. He simply stayed faithful, in small ways, day after day, for a whole lifetime, in a place that was not his home. He was kind to his enemies. He told the truth to the powerful. He kept his prayers, and his windows open toward home. And the book says that he was greatly beloved.

Perhaps some of that can be true of us as well, in our own small ways, in whatever place we find ourselves tonight.

Now let the story go. Let the river carry it away, slowly, downstream, toward the sea. Let your breathing slow. Let your hands rest. The lamps of Babylon are going out one by one. The lions are sleeping. The stars are coming out over the flat land between the rivers, steady and bright.

You have done enough today. You do not need to understand everything. You can go your way now, and rest.

The Lord bless you, and keep you. The Lord make his face to shine on you, and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up his face toward you, and give you peace.

Goodnight. Sleep well.
