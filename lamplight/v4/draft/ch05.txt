## Chapter 5: A Fugitive in the Wilderness

Welcome back. Let yourself sink a little deeper. The story is going to wander now, the way David wandered, from one hiding place to another, through some of the loneliest country in the Bible.

When David left Jonathan in the field, he had nothing. No soldiers, no food, no weapon. He went first to a little town called Nob, where the priests of the Lord lived, and he came to the priest Ahimelech. Ahimelech was nervous to see him alone. David told him he was on the king's business, a secret errand, and asked for bread.

The priest had no ordinary bread. He had only the holy bread, the loaves that had been set out before the Lord and then replaced with fresh ones. He gave them to David. And when David asked for a weapon, the priest said there was only one. The sword of Goliath, the Philistine you killed in the valley of Elah. It is here, wrapped in a cloth, behind the ephod. There is no other.

There is none like that, said David. Give it to me.

And so he went on, with holy bread in his bag and a giant's sword at his side.

The story does not hide what came of that visit. One of Saul's servants, an Edomite named Doeg, the chief of Saul's herdsmen, was there that day and saw it all. Later he told the king. And Saul, in his fear and anger, ordered the priests of Nob to be put to death. It is one of the darkest moments in the whole account. One son of Ahimelech, a young priest named Abiathar, escaped and fled to David. And David said to him, I knew that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul. Stay with me. Do not be afraid. Whoever seeks my life seeks yours, and with me you will be safe. Abiathar stayed with David for the rest of his life.

David tried everything. He even went to Gath, one of the five cities of the Philistines, the hometown of Goliath himself, hoping that among Israel's enemies Saul would not reach him. But the servants of the Philistine king recognised him. Is this not David, they said, the one they sang about in their dances? Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands? David was very much afraid. So he pretended to be mad. He scratched on the doors of the gate and let his spittle run down into his beard, and the king of Gath said to his servants, do I lack madmen, that you have brought this fellow to play the madman in front of me? And they let him go.

From there he went east, into the hills, to a cave near a town called Adullam.

{{1SA 22:1-2}}

Four hundred men. Not an army of heroes. A company of the troubled, the indebted and the bitter, the people who had nowhere else to go. And the shepherd became their captain. David took his father and mother across the Jordan to the king of Moab and asked him to let them stay there in safety until he knew what God would do for him. Perhaps he remembered that his great-grandmother Ruth had come from Moab.

Over the months and years that followed, David and his men moved through the wilderness of Judah. It is a strange and beautiful country, if you have ever seen it. East of Bethlehem and Hebron, the land falls away toward the Dead Sea in long bare ridges of chalk and limestone, cut by deep dry gorges. There are almost no trees. In winter, after rain, a thin green covers the hills for a few weeks, and then it is gone. The rock is full of caves. The days are hot and bright, the nights are cold, and the stars are very close. Down near the sea there are a few springs, where water comes out of the cliffs and makes small green places among the rocks.

The names in this part of the story are the names of hiding places. Keilah, a town that David rescued from Philistine raiders, only to learn that the townspeople would hand him over to Saul if he stayed. The wilderness of Ziph. The strongholds in the hill country. The wilderness of Maon. Again and again, someone told Saul where David was, and Saul came with his soldiers, and David slipped away just in time.

{{1SA 23:14}}

And once, in the middle of all this running, a visitor came to him in secret, at a place called Horesh in the wilderness of Ziph. It was Jonathan.

{{1SA 23:16-18}}

It is the last time the two friends would ever see each other. They did not know it then. They made their promise again, before the Lord, and then the prince went home, and David stayed in the wilderness.

Not long afterwards, Saul came so close that the two companies were on opposite sides of the same mountain. Saul and his men were closing in around David's men to take them. And then a messenger arrived, out of breath, with news for the king. Hurry and come, for the Philistines have made a raid on the land. And Saul turned back from chasing David to go and fight the Philistines. The old writers say that the place got a name from that day, which means the Rock of Escape, or the Rock of Parting.

Many readers have heard David's own voice in the Psalms from this time. Some of the Psalms carry little headings that connect them with these years. One is described as a prayer when he was in the cave. Another, Psalm fifty-seven, is headed "when he fled from Saul, in the cave". It is a quiet song, for all its danger. Let me read you a few lines of it.

In the shadow of your wings. That is a picture worth holding on to tonight. A small bird under its mother's wing, sheltered while the storm goes by. A tired man in a cave, with his back against the cool rock, and four hundred others sleeping around him in the dark, waiting for the trouble to pass.

Let the wilderness be quiet now. The cave is dim and cool. Outside, the stars are very bright over the bare ridges, and down below, somewhere, a spring is trickling into a small green pool among the stones.
