## Chapter 4: The Bush That Was Not Consumed

Let us go out now into the wilderness, with Moses and his sheep.

One day, after many long years in Midian, Moses led the flock further than usual, far across the desert, to the back of the wilderness, as the old words say, until he came to a mountain called Horeb. In other places, this same mountain is called Sinai. It was a quiet, empty place of bare red rock and thin, dry scrub, where the wind moved over the stones, and the silence was very deep.

And there, something happened that would change everything.

Now Moses was keeping the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian, and he led the flock to the back of the wilderness, and came to God's mountain, to Horeb. The Lord's angel appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the middle of a bush. He looked, and behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.

Let us picture it slowly. A thorny desert bush, the kind that grows among the rocks, dry and grey. And in the middle of it, fire. Not a fire that crackled and blackened the branches and burned out in a minute, as a dry bush would. A fire that went on burning, steadily, gently, and yet the bush was not burned up. The leaves stayed green. The branches stayed whole.

Moses was curious. Moses said, I will go now, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burned.

And then, out of the fire, came a voice, calling his name.

When the Lord saw that he came over to see, God called to him out of the middle of the bush, and said, Moses! Moses! He said, Here I am. He said, Don't come close. Take off your sandals, for the place you are standing on is holy ground. Moreover he said, I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. Moses hid his face because he was afraid to look at God.

Let us imagine Moses bending down in the dust, untying his worn leather sandals, and setting them aside, and standing barefoot on the warm stones. The ordinary ground of the desert, where he had walked a thousand times, had become holy, simply because God was there.

Then God spoke to him about his people.

I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters, for I know their sorrows. I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land to a good and large land, to a land flowing with milk and honey.

I know their sorrows. Those are tender words. And then came the call.

Come now therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh, that you may bring my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt.

Moses was not eager. He was not the young prince any more, full of fire and certainty. He was an old shepherd now, about eighty years old, and he knew his own weakness. Who am I, he said, that I should go to Pharaoh, and that I should bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?

And God gave him the simplest of answers. Certainly I will be with you.

Moses had more questions. If he went to the people, and they asked him the name of the God who had sent him, what should he say? And God answered with words that people have pondered ever since. I am who I am. Tell them, I am has sent you. It is a name that seems to mean, the one who is, the one who will be, the one who is always present. It is the root of the holy name that the old text uses again and again, the name that we are saying tonight as the Lord.

Still Moses was afraid. What if they don't believe me, he asked. So God gave him signs. The shepherd's staff in his hand became a snake when he threw it on the ground, and Moses ran from it, and then, when he caught it by the tail, it became a staff again. His hand, put inside his cloak, came out white, and then whole again.

And still Moses hesitated. He said, O Lord, I am not eloquent, neither before now, nor since you have spoken to your servant; for I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.

There is something very human about Moses here. He is not a fearless hero. He is a man who does not like speaking in public, who feels he is the wrong person, and who says so, honestly. God answered him gently at first. Who made man's mouth? Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.

But Moses said, Oh, Lord, please send someone else.

And so God gave him a helper. His older brother, Aaron, who could speak well, would go with him, and speak for him. Aaron, God said, was already on his way to meet him, and he would be glad in his heart to see him.

Moses went back to Jethro, and asked his blessing to return to Egypt, and the old priest said, simply, Go in peace. So Moses set his wife and sons on a donkey, and took the shepherd's staff in his hand, the staff that would now be called the rod of God, and he set out on the long road west. Somewhere in the wilderness, on the mountain of God, his brother Aaron came out to meet him, and kissed him. The two of them, the shepherd and the speaker, went on together to Egypt.

Let us leave them there, two brothers walking side by side across the darkening desert, a donkey plodding beside them, the mountain behind them fading into the dusk, and somewhere back among the rocks, a quiet bush, still green, still whole, as if it were waiting.
