## Chapter 12: A Shining Face, a Tent of Meeting, and Goodnight

We have come to the last chapter of our story. Let us make ourselves comfortable, and let the pace grow slower still.

While Moses was up on the mountain, inside the cloud, the days went by in the camp below. Ten days. Twenty. Thirty. Forty. And the people grew restless and afraid. Moses had vanished into the fire and the darkness, and he had not come back.

When the people saw that Moses delayed coming down from the mountain, the people gathered themselves together to Aaron, and said to him, Come, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has become of him.

And Aaron, the brother who had stood beside Moses through everything, gave way. He asked the people for their golden earrings, and they brought them, and he melted down the gold and made from it the image of a calf. And the people said, These are your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt. They held a feast, and danced around it.

It is a sad and very human part of the story. So soon after the sea, so soon after the mountain of fire, they forgot. Frightened people often reach for something they can see and touch.

God told Moses what was happening, and his anger burned. But Moses pleaded for the people. He reminded God of his promises to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and begged him to turn from his anger. So the Lord turned away from the evil which he said he would do to his people.

When Moses came down and saw the calf and the dancing, he was so grieved and angry that he threw down the stone tablets he was carrying, and they broke at the foot of the mountain. He burned the calf and ground it to powder. It was a hard and sorrowful time in the camp, and the old story tells of a day of judgement in which many people died. We will not dwell on it tonight. But the scripture is honest that the road to faithfulness was not smooth.

And then, Moses went back up the mountain, to pray for the people again.

There is a lovely verse from these days. The Lord spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend. Moses had once been a frightened shepherd, hiding his face at a burning bush. Now he spoke with God as a friend.

Moses asked to see God's glory. And God answered that no one could see his face and live, but that he would let his goodness pass by. He placed Moses in a cleft of the rock, and covered him with his hand, and passed by.

So Moses cut two new tablets of stone, like the first ones, and early in the morning he climbed the mountain again, alone, carrying them in his arms. And there, in the cloud, God passed by before him, and spoke his own name, and told Moses what kind of God he is. Of all the words in the book of Exodus, these may be the most important. Let us hear them slowly.

The Lord! The Lord, a merciful and gracious God, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness and truth, keeping loving kindness for thousands, forgiving iniquity and disobedience and sin.

Merciful and gracious. Slow to anger. Abundant in loving kindness. After the golden calf, after all their complaining and forgetting, this is how God described himself. Moses hurried, and bowed his head toward the earth, and worshipped.

When Moses came down from the mountain this time, carrying the new tablets, something had happened to him that he did not know.

When Moses came down from Mount Sinai with the two tablets of the covenant in Moses' hand, Moses didn't know that the skin of his face shone by reason of his speaking with him.

His face was shining. He had been so long in the presence of God that some of the light had stayed on him. The people were afraid to come near at first, but Moses called them, and spoke with them. And afterwards, he wore a veil over his face, taking it off only when he went in to speak with the Lord.

Then the people began a work that brought them together. They built a tabernacle, a beautiful tent of meeting, a holy place that could be carried with them wherever they went. Everyone gave what they could. Gold and silver and bronze. Fine linen, and blue and purple and scarlet yarn. Goats' hair, and rams' skins, and acacia wood. Skilled workers carved and wove and stitched. Women spun the yarn with their own hands. So much was brought that in the end Moses had to ask them to stop giving.

At last, on the first day of the first month, almost a year after they had left Egypt, the tabernacle was set up in the middle of the camp.

Then the cloud covered the Tent of Meeting, and the Lord's glory filled the tabernacle. When the cloud was taken up from over the tabernacle, the children of Israel went onward, throughout all their journeys; but if the cloud wasn't taken up, then they didn't travel until the day that it was taken up. For the cloud of the Lord was on the tabernacle by day, and there was fire in the cloud by night, in the sight of all the house of Israel, throughout all their journeys.

And that is where the book of Exodus ends. Not in the promised land. Not at the end of the journey. But in the middle of the wilderness, with God dwelling among his people, in a tent, in the centre of the camp. The long story of the road would go on, through other books, for forty years. But here, for now, they rest.

Let us leave them there, one last time. A camp of tents spread out across a quiet plain beneath a great dark mountain. In the middle of it all, a tent of meeting, its curtains still. Above it, the cloud, and inside the cloud, a soft and steady fire, glowing gently through the whole night, like a lamp left burning for a sleeping household. Around it, thousands of families asleep. A people who were once slaves, now free, and not alone.

Thank you for walking with me tonight, all the way from a basket among the reeds to a lamp in the wilderness. Let your breathing slow. Let your hands rest. Whatever the road ahead of you holds, you do not have to walk all of it tonight. There is nothing more to do now. The lamp is lit, and the night is quiet, and long.

The Lord bless you, and keep you.

Goodnight. Sleep well.
