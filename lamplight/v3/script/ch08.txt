## Chapter 8: A Path Through the Sea

Let us walk now with the people of Israel, out into the edge of the wilderness.

There was a short road from Egypt to the land of Canaan, along the coast of the sea, through the country of the Philistines. But God did not lead them that way. The old words give the reason. God said, Lest perhaps the people change their minds when they see war, and they return to Egypt. These were people who had been slaves all their lives. They were not ready for battles. So God led them the long way round, by the way of the wilderness, toward the Red Sea.

And God did not leave them to find the way alone.

The Lord went before them by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them on their way, and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light, that they might go by day and by night.

Let us picture it. By day, a tall column of cloud, moving slowly ahead of the people across the desert, so that everyone, even the smallest child at the back, could see which way to go. Perhaps it gave them shade from the hard sun as well. And at night, the same column glowing like fire, lighting up the camp, a great soft lamp standing over the sleeping tents. Day and night, it did not leave them.

They came to a place beside the sea, and there they camped. The scripture names the spot, Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea. Nobody today knows for certain exactly where it was, or exactly which body of water they crossed. The old Hebrew name means the Sea of Reeds. But wherever it was, it was a dead end. The water was in front of them, and the desert all around.

And back in Egypt, Pharaoh changed his mind one last time.

He looked at his empty building sites, and his silent brickyards, and he said, What have we done, letting Israel go from serving us? And he ordered his chariot made ready. He took six hundred of his finest chariots, and all the other chariots of Egypt, with officers over all of them, and he set out in pursuit.

When Pharaoh came near, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes, and behold, the Egyptians were marching after them; and they were very afraid.

Let us imagine that moment. The people camped by the shore, cooking, resting, children playing at the water's edge. And then someone looking back toward the west, and seeing a line of dust on the horizon. And then the glint of sun on metal, and the dark shapes of horses and chariots, coming on.

Panic spread through the camp. People turned on Moses. Were there no graves in Egypt, they cried, that you have brought us out here to die in the wilderness? It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians.

And Moses, the man who had once said he was slow of speech, spoke some of the calmest and bravest words in the whole Bible.

Don't be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the Lord, which he will work for you today; for you will never again see the Egyptians whom you have seen today. The Lord will fight for you, and you shall be still.

The Lord will fight for you, and you shall be still. Let those words rest with you for a moment. There are times when there is nothing more we can do, and the bravest thing is simply to be still, and trust.

Then the pillar of cloud moved. It rose from the front of the camp and went and stood behind them, between the people of Israel and the army of Egypt. To the Egyptians it was darkness. To Israel it gave light. And all that night, the two camps could not come near each other.

Then God told Moses to lift up his staff and stretch out his hand over the sea.

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the Lord caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided. The children of Israel went into the middle of the sea on the dry ground; and the waters were a wall to them on their right hand and on their left.

Let us picture it, slowly. A great wind blowing all night long, out of the east, roaring over the water. And the sea drawing back, and back, and opening. A path appearing across the seabed, wet sand and stones glistening under the fire of the pillar. Walls of dark water standing on either side. And the people walking down into it. Families holding hands. Mothers carrying babies. Old men leaning on their sticks. Sheep and goats picking their way across the stones. All night long they walked, between the waters, with the wind in their hair, and the light of the pillar of fire behind them.

In the morning, as the last of them came up onto the far shore, the Egyptian chariots came after them, down into the path through the sea. But their wheels clogged and twisted in the mud, and they drove heavily, and the soldiers grew afraid. Let's flee, they cried.

Then Moses stretched out his hand again over the sea, and as morning appeared, the waters came back to their place, and covered the chariots and the horsemen, all of Pharaoh's army that had followed them into the sea.

It is a hard and fearful ending for those soldiers, and the old story does not hide it. But it was also the end of a long, long night of slavery. The people who had been hunted stood safe on the far shore, in the morning light, and the sea lay calm between them and Egypt.

Thus the Lord saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians. Israel saw the great work which the Lord did, and the people feared the Lord; and they believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses.

Let us leave them there, on the eastern shore, in the early morning. The wind has dropped. The sea is quiet again, small waves turning over softly on the sand. Thousands of tired people are sitting on the beach, holding one another, hardly able to believe it. They are free.
