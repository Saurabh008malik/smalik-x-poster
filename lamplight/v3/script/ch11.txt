## Chapter 11: The Mountain of Fire, and Ten Words

Let us come now, with the people of Israel, to the foot of the holy mountain.

In the third month after they left Egypt, the people came into the wilderness of Sinai, and they camped there, in front of the mountain. It was the same mountain where, long before, an old shepherd had seen a bush burning in the rocks. God had told Moses then that when he brought the people out of Egypt, they would worship God on this mountain. And now here they were.

Let us picture the place. A great mountain of granite, red and grey and brown, rising steep and bare out of the desert, its peaks catching the first and last light of every day. Below it, a wide plain where the tents of Israel spread out in their thousands, with the smoke of cooking fires going up in the still air. Silence all around, the deep silence of the high desert.

Moses went up the mountain to God. And God gave him a message for the people, full of tenderness.

You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings, and brought you to myself. Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, then you shall be my own possession from among all peoples; for all the earth is mine; and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.

I bore you on eagles' wings. It is a beautiful picture. In the old stories, a mother eagle teaching her young ones to fly would spread her great wings beneath them and carry them up when they faltered. That is how God described the long road from Egypt. Not as a hard march, but as being carried.

Moses came down and told the people, and all the people answered together, All that the Lord has spoken we will do. Then they spent three days making themselves ready. They washed their clothes, and they prepared their hearts. And a boundary was set around the foot of the mountain, so that no one would go up or touch it.

On the third day, in the morning, it began.

On the third day, when it was morning, there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud on the mountain, and the sound of an exceedingly loud trumpet; and all the people who were in the camp trembled. Moses led the people out of the camp to meet God; and they stood at the lower part of the mountain. All of Mount Sinai smoked, because the Lord descended on it in fire; and its smoke ascended like the smoke of a furnace, and the whole mountain quaked greatly.

It was an awesome sight, and the scripture does not make it gentle. Thunder rolling among the peaks. Lightning flashing in the cloud. The mountain wrapped in smoke and fire, trembling under their feet. And the sound of a trumpet, growing louder and louder.

And then God spoke. These are the words that are often called the Ten Commandments. In the old Hebrew, they are called simply the ten words.

They begin, not with a command, but with a reminder of who God is, and what he has done.

I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.

And then the words themselves. Let us hear them slowly, as the people heard them that morning, standing at the foot of the mountain.

You shall have no other gods before me.

You shall not make for yourselves an idol, nor any image of anything that is in the heavens above, or that is on the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth: you shall not bow yourself down to them, nor serve them.

You shall not misuse the name of the Lord your God.

Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. You shall labor six days, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the Lord your God. You shall not do any work in it, you, nor your son, nor your daughter, your male servant, nor your female servant, nor your livestock, nor your stranger who is within your gates.

Honor your father and your mother, that your days may be long in the land which the Lord your God gives you.

You shall not murder.

You shall not commit adultery.

You shall not steal.

You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor.

You shall not covet your neighbor's house. You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, nor his male servant, nor his female servant, nor his ox, nor his donkey, nor anything that is your neighbor's.

Let us rest with the Sabbath commandment for a moment. These people had been slaves. For four hundred years they had worked without rest, day after day, for masters who never let them stop. And now, one of the very first gifts of their freedom was a day of rest. One day in every seven, when no one would work, not the master, not the servant, not the stranger, not even the animals. A day to stop, and breathe, and remember that they were free.

The people were frightened by the thunder and the fire. They stood far off, and they said to Moses, You speak with us, and we will listen, but don't let God speak with us, or we will die. And Moses said to them, Don't be afraid.

The people stayed at a distance, and Moses came near to the thick darkness where God was.

Moses went up the mountain, into the cloud, and the scripture says he stayed there forty days and forty nights. There he received many more laws and teachings for the people. Laws about justice and kindness. Laws protecting the widow and the orphan, the poor and the stranger. You shall not oppress an alien, God told them, for you know the heart of an alien, since you were aliens in the land of Egypt.

Many of these laws are surprisingly gentle, and they show a kind of care for the small and the vulnerable that was rare in the ancient world. Let us hear two or three of them.

If a poor man gave you his cloak as a pledge for a loan, you were to give it back to him before the sun went down.

If you take your neighbor's garment as collateral, you shall restore it to him before the sun goes down, for that is his only covering, it is his garment for his skin. What would he sleep in? It will happen, when he cries to me, that I will hear, for I am gracious.

What would he sleep in? It is such a tender question, to find in a book of laws. God cared whether a poor man had a blanket at night.

And even an enemy was to be helped.

If you meet your enemy's ox or his donkey going astray, you shall surely bring it back to him again. If you see the donkey of him who hates you fallen down under his burden, don't leave him. You shall surely help him with it.

And the day of rest was for everyone, even the tired animals.

Six days you shall do your work, and on the seventh day you shall rest, that your ox and your donkey may have rest, and the son of your servant, and the alien may be refreshed.

Refreshed. Let that word settle over you tonight. Rest is not only allowed. It is a gift, meant for everyone, and for every creature.

Let us leave them there, at the foot of the mountain, on a quiet night. The thunder has gone. The fire on the peak has faded into a soft glow inside the cloud. In the camp below, the people lie in their tents, thinking about ten simple words, while far above them, somewhere in the cloud, an old man is listening.
