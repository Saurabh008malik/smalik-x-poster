## Chapter 3: A Garden in the East

The second chapter of Genesis tells the story of the beginning again, but closer up. The first telling was wide, like looking at the whole earth from very far away. This telling is near. It is about one garden, one man, one woman, and the quiet of an evening when the air is cool.

The story says that in the day the Lord God made the earth and the heavens, there was not yet any shrub of the field, and no plant had sprouted, because it had not rained, and there was no one to work the ground. But a mist went up from the earth and watered the whole surface of it. Picture that. A world before rain. Only a soft mist rising out of the ground each morning, settling on everything, keeping it damp.

And then the Lord God formed man from the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living soul.

The old Hebrew has a gentle joke in it that gets lost in English. The word for man is adam, and the word for ground is adamah. The man is the earth creature, the one made of the soil. He is dust that breathes. That is all any of us are, the story says, and it does not say it sadly. Dust that has been breathed into is a wonderful thing.

Then the Lord God planted a garden, eastward, in a place called Eden, and put the man there. Out of the ground he made every tree grow that is pleasant to the sight and good for food. Notice the order. Pleasant to look at first. Good to eat second. The garden was beautiful before it was useful. And in the middle of the garden stood two trees with names. The tree of life, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

A river went out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it divided and became four rivers. The story names them. Pishon, which wound through a land where there was gold, and the gold of that land was good, and there was bdellium and the onyx stone. Gihon, which flowed around the land of Cush. Hiddekel, which is the Tigris, flowing east of Assyria. And the fourth river was the Euphrates.

You do not need to remember the names. I only read them because there is something soothing about a list of rivers. Pishon. Gihon. Tigris. Euphrates. Four streams of water running out from one quiet place, carrying the garden's freshness into the wider world.

Here are the old words about the man's work there.

The Lord God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it. The Lord God commanded the man, saying, "Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, you shall not eat of it; for in the day that you eat of it you will surely die."

So the man was given a job and a boundary. The job was to tend the garden and to keep it, to be its gardener and its guard. The boundary was one tree. Every other tree in the place was freely his. The emphasis in the old words is on freedom. You may freely eat. Of every tree. There was more than he could ever need, and only a single thing withheld.

Then the story says something that may surprise you, if you remember how often the first chapter said that things were good. The Lord God said, it is not good that the man should be alone. This is the first time in the Bible that anything is called not good. Not the darkness, not the deep waters, not the wild animals. Loneliness. That was the first thing the maker looked at and would not accept.

So he said, I will make him a helper suitable for him. And out of the ground he formed every animal of the field and every bird of the sky, and brought them to the man to see what he would call them. Whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name. Imagine that long afternoon. The man sitting in the grass, and the animals coming one by one, and the man looking at each one and finding its name. Ox. Dove. Fox. Each one was good company for a while. But among them all there was not found a helper suitable for him.

So the Lord God caused a deep sleep to fall on the man, and he slept. That is worth saying twice tonight. A deep sleep, sent as a gift, so that something good could be made while he rested. While the man slept, God took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh in its place, and from the rib he made a woman, and brought her to the man.

And the man woke, and saw her, and said the first words any human being speaks in the Bible. They are a kind of poem. This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh. She will be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.

The chapter ends with a line that is read at weddings all over the world. Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh. And then the last sentence, which is the quietest of all. They were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed.

That is the garden before anything went wrong. Two people, a river, trees heavy with fruit, animals with new names, and no shame anywhere. The evening breeze moving through the leaves. It is the picture the whole rest of the Bible keeps looking back toward.

Hold it for a moment. The cool of the day. The sound of water dividing into four streams. Someone sleeping deeply in the grass, and waking to find they are no longer alone.

When we continue, a voice will come into the garden that does not belong there. But that is for later. For now, the gate is still open, and the fruit is still on the tree, and the two of them are still unafraid.
