## Chapter 2: The Lamps in the Sky, and the Day of Rest

On the fourth day, God set lights in the sky.

Until now there had been light, but no lamps. Light without a source, like the glow before dawn when you cannot yet see the sun. On the fourth day the story says that God made two great lights, a greater light to rule the day, and a lesser light to rule the night. He made the stars also. The old text almost adds the stars as an afterthought, as if to say, and the stars too, all of them, the whole uncountable field of them, in the same breath.

The lights were given work to do. They were to divide the day from the night, and to be for signs and for seasons, for days and for years. That is to say, they were the first clock and the first calendar. Every harvest ever planned, every sabbath ever kept, every birthday ever remembered, was going to be counted by the slow turning of these lamps. And God saw that it was good. And there was evening, and there was morning, a fourth day.

Here are the old words, slowly.

God said, "Let there be lights in the expanse of sky to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days and years; and let them be for lights in the expanse of sky to give light on the earth;" and it was so. God made the two great lights: the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night. He also made the stars. God set them in the expanse of sky to give light to the earth, and to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness. God saw that it was good. There was evening and there was morning, a fourth day.

The moon is the lesser light, the one that rules the night. If you have ever lain awake and watched moonlight move across a floor, you have been under the rule of that lesser lamp. It is a gentle government. It asks nothing of you. It only lights the room enough that you are not alone in it.

On the fifth day, the waters were filled. God said, let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the face of the sky. And so the seas, which had been empty since they were gathered, were suddenly thick with life. Great sea creatures, the enormous slow shapes that move in the deep. Every kind of fish. And overhead, every winged bird, each after its kind. And God saw that it was good, and this time he did something new. He blessed them. Be fruitful, he said, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let the birds multiply on the earth. The first blessing in the Bible was spoken to fish and birds. And there was evening, and there was morning, a fifth day.

On the sixth day, the land was filled. Cattle, and creeping things, and the wild animals of the earth, each after its kind. Picture them arriving, the deer and the oxen and the lions, the small lizards in the sun, the beetles under the leaves, all of it, called up out of the ground as the plants had been.

And then, on the same day, the story slows down. God said, let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And God created humankind in his own image. In the image of God he created them. Male and female he created them. And God blessed them, and told them to be fruitful and to multiply and to fill the earth, and to care for it, and for every living thing that moved on it. He gave them the green plants and the fruit of the trees for food. And God looked at everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good.

Not just good this time. Very good. The whole, finished, filled creation, seen all at once, with people in it.

Here are the old words for the end of the sixth day.

God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. There was evening and there was morning, a sixth day.

And then comes the seventh day. I want to read it to you directly, because it is one of the quietest passages in the whole Bible, and it belongs to the night.

The heavens and the earth were finished, and all their vast array. On the seventh day God finished his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made. God blessed the seventh day, and made it holy, because he rested in it from all his work which he had created and made.

God rested. Not because he was tired, the old teachers would say, but because the work was complete, and completion deserves rest. The seventh day is the only day in the story that is not followed by the words, and there was evening and there was morning. It has no ending written into it. Some readers over the centuries have loved that detail. They say the seventh day is still going on. The rest has never closed.

So here is where you are, as you lie in the dark. You are inside the seventh day. The sky has its lamps. The seas are full. The land is green and crowded with living things. The people have been made and blessed. And the maker has stopped working, and looked at all of it, and called it very good, and sat down to rest.

You may rest too. There is nothing left on the list tonight.

When we go on, we will walk into a garden, planted in the east, with a river running through it. But there is no hurry. The garden has been waiting a long time. It can wait a little longer while you breathe.
