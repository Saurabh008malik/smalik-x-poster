## Chapter 1: Welcome, and the First Evening

Good evening, and welcome. You can put the day down now. Whatever it held, it is finished, and it does not need you anymore. Let the room be dim. Let your shoulders be heavy. There is nothing to do tonight but listen, and you do not even have to do that very well.

This is Lamplight Scripture, and tonight we are going to walk slowly through the opening of the Bible, the book of Genesis. We will begin at the very beginning, with the creation of the world, and we will travel as far as the story carries us before you sleep, through the garden, the flood, the tower, and the long road of Abraham. You do not need to stay awake for all of it. The stories will keep going quietly whether you are listening or not, like a river that does not mind who is watching.

A few words about how this night will go. I will tell the stories the way you might tell them to someone you love, in plain and gentle language, taking my time. And now and then I will read the old words themselves, from a public domain translation called the World English Bible, so that you hear the scripture in its own voice. When I read the scripture, I will go slowly. You can let the words wash over you. Nothing here is meant to teach you or correct you. It is only meant to keep you company in the dark.

So let us begin where everything begins.

Before there was a morning, before there was a sea or a shore or a single blade of grass, there was God. That is the first thing the book says, and it says it without any fuss, the way you might say that the sun is warm. In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

Try to picture the earth as the story first describes it. It was formless and empty. There was no shape to anything yet. There was deep water, and over the water there was darkness, and over the darkness, the Spirit of God was moving, hovering, the way a bird hovers over its nest. That is the whole of the first picture. Water, darkness, and a presence that is not afraid of the dark.

Here are the old words.

In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty. Darkness was on the surface of the deep. God's Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters. God said, "Let there be light," and there was light. God saw the light, and saw that it was good. God divided the light from the darkness. God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. There was evening and there was morning, one day.

Notice how the first day is counted. Not morning and then evening, but evening and then morning. The day begins in the dark. In the old way of counting, the night was not the end of the day, it was the start of it. Rest came first, and the work came after. I find that a kind thought to carry into sleep. You are not falling out of the day tonight. You are beginning the next one, the way the first day began, in a quiet darkness that is about to be called good.

On the second day, the story says, God spoke again, and made a wide space in the middle of the waters. He called that space sky. Now there were waters below, which would become the seas, and waters above, which the ancient people imagined as the great blue vault overhead, holding back the rain. And there was evening, and there was morning, a second day.

On the third day, the waters below were gathered together into one place, and dry land appeared. God called the dry land Earth, and the gathered waters he called Seas. And God saw that it was good. Then, on the same day, the earth was asked to bring forth growing things. Grass, and herbs with seeds, and trees with fruit, each kind carrying its own seed inside it, so that the green world could go on renewing itself long after that first afternoon. And God saw that it was good. And there was evening, and there was morning, a third day.

I want you to rest for a moment in that third day. Imagine standing on new ground, soft and dark and damp, with the sea pulling back from it for the first time. Imagine the first green shoots coming up, slow as breath. The first tree. The first apple, or fig, or olive, swelling on a branch that had never carried fruit before. Nobody was there to see it. There were no eyes yet. There was only the quiet pleasure of the maker, looking at what had been made, and calling it good.

Three times now the story has said that word. Good. Not perfect, not finished, just good. It is the word a carpenter uses when a joint fits, or a cook uses when the bread has risen. It is a word of contentment. And it is going to be said again, and again, until the whole world is covered in it.

That is where we will pause for now, with the land green and the seas in their place and the sky stretched out overhead, waiting for lights. The rest of the week is still ahead of us. The sun has not yet been made. There is no need to hurry toward it.

Breathe out slowly. Let the bed hold you. In a moment we will go on to the fourth day, when the lamps were hung in the sky.
